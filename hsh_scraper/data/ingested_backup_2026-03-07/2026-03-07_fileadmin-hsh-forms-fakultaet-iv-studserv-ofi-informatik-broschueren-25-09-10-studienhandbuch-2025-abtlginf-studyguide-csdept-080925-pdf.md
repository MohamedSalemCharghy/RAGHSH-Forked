**2.2 Studienaufbau**

Die Studiengänge der Abteilung Informatik sind
modular aufgebaut. Ein _Modul_ ist gleichbedeutend mit
einer oder mehreren Lehrveranstaltungen.


Ein Modul wird von den Studierenden in der Regel
innerhalb eines Semesters absolviert - eine
Auflistung aller Module, die in der Abteilung
angeboten werden, ist im Modulverzeichnis (online)
enthalten.


Studierende absolvieren während des Studiums in
allen Lehrveranstaltungen eines Moduls
Leistungsnachweise in Form von _Prüfungen_ .
Leistungsnachweise werden also studienbegleitend –
und nicht am Ende des Studiums – erbracht.
Studierende haben ein Modul bestanden, wenn sie
alle Prüfungsleistungen zu einem Modul bestanden
haben.


Jedem Modul ist eine Anzahl von _Credits_ (CR, auch
Leistungs- oder Kreditpunkte) als Maß für den
durchschnittlichen Studienaufwand zugeordnet sowie
eine Anzahl von _Semesterwochenstunden (SWS)_ als
Maß der Präsenzstunden pro Studienwoche (siehe
Modulverzeichnis).


Die Credits dienen den Studierenden zur Messung
ihres Studienfortschritts. Mit jedem vollständig
absolvierten Modul werden den Studierenden die
dem Modul zugeordneten Credits zugeschrieben.
Alle Studiengänge sind so ausgelegt, dass pro
Semester durchschnittlich 30 Credits erworben
werden müssen, um das Studium innerhalb der
Regelstudienzeit abzuschließen.



Die Fakultät arbeitet mit vielen Institutionen im
Ausland zusammen. Im Zuge dieser
Zusammenarbeit werden etwa gemeinsame
Lehrveranstaltungen angeboten und Studierende und
Dozenten/innen ausgetauscht. Wenn Studierende
einen Teil ihres Studiums im Ausland verbringen
wollen, werden sie von der Fakultät vielfältig
unterstützt. Für Auslandstudium und –praktika steht
ein spezieller Ansprechpartner bereit.


Für alle Fragen, die rund um das Studium entstehen
und für die nicht unmittelbar ein Ansprechpartner
erkennbar ist, kann die Studienberatung genutzt
werden. Dort werden diese Fragen entweder
beantwortet oder an die zuständige Person
weitergeleitet.


Mit der Anzahl der Credits für ein Modul lässt sich
auch der relative Arbeitsaufwand für Studierende
abschätzen, der zum erfolgreichen Absolvieren eines
Moduls eingesetzt werden muss.


Die Credits dienen ferner der Anerkennung von
Prüfungsleistungen bei einem Wechsel des
Studienortes nach dem European Credit
Transfersystem (ECTS).


An der Angabe der Semesterwochenstunden für
Lehrveranstaltungen ist abzulesen, wie viele Stunden
pro Woche die Studierenden durchschnittlich in
Lehrveranstaltungen in der Hochschule verbringen

absolvieren zu können. Darüber hinaus sind von den
Studierenden Zeiten für Vor- und Nachbereitungen
sowie Prüfungsvorbereitungen vorzusehen.

**2.2 Study structure**

The course of studies for the study programs of the
Department of Computer Science is modular in
design. One _module_ can mean either one or several
courses.


Usually a module can be completed in one semester,
i. e. all courses of a module take place simultaneously in one semester. A list of all the modules
offered by the department can be accessed in the
Module Directory (online).


Students are required to take _examinations_ to
complete all the courses in a module. This means that
their progress is constantly monitored during their
study time – and not just for the final degree. Students have passed a module when they have
successfully completed all the examinations for that
module.


A number of _credits_ (CR) is assigned to each module
to reflect the study effort made as well as a number of
_semester week hours (SWS)_ to reflect student
presence per study week (see Module Directory).


The credits help the students to monitor their own
progress. With each module they successfully
complete, they are awarded that number of credits.
All the courses of study are designed so that an
average of 30 credits must be achieved per semester
to successfully complete the study program within the
prescribed time.


The number of credits for a module reflects the
amount of effort a student must invest in order to
successfully complete the module.


The credits also mean that any studies completed can
be transferred to any other university or college in



training and are usually coordinated with an outside
company.

**2.3 International offers**

Internationality is becoming increasingly important in
the professional life of a computer scientist. The
challenges presented range from the personal to
virtual interaction with colleagues who speak different
languages to the development of software and
documentation in English and right on up to actual
work assignments in the American subsidiary of a
company or at an Asian customer’s, for example.


The Department of Computer Science has followed
this development and actively promotes the internationalization of the university. It encourages its
students to gather as much international experience
as possible during their studies. The most important
goals include:


- Active knowledge of English and other modern
foreign languages

- Confidence in reading, understanding and writing
specialized texts in English

**3. Master Study Program in Computer Science (MIN)**


**3.1 Goal and Contents of the Study Program**



The application-oriented Master study program in
_Computer_ _Science_ teaches more in-depth,
specialized knowledge in selected fields of computer
science and enables graduates to apply scientific
methods and findings to difficult, complex problems in
actual practice as well as in research. The goals of
the Master study program fundamentally reflect the
goals formulated in the GI Recommendations for
Master Study Programs. The study program is
designed to qualify graduates for the following tasks
in particular:


- Executive functions in information technology,

- Activities in computer science research and

- Upper-level positions for careers in the civil
service.


The Master study program is the consecutive
continuation of the Bachelor study program of the
same name. The Master program focuses on
advanced courses in the theoretical and the
application-oriented computer science subjects. The
Master study program has been designed with two
goals in mind – specialization and regional
connections. Specialization is achieved by providing
advanced courses in specific subjects that are in
particularly high demand in the field of computer
science. Students can currently choose courses from
four forward-looking areas of specialization (majors):
_Computer Graphics_, _Data Science_, _IT Security_ as well
as _Complex Software Systems_ . In the areas of
specialization, a selection of the following courses is
offered each semester:


The many positive statements made by regional as
well as national companies indicate that there are
currently, and will be in the future, many opportunities
for qualified jobs in these fields – in Greater Hannover
as well as throughout Germany.


The major _Computer Graphics_ deals with the topics
of real-time 3D computer graphics (real-time
rendering), geometric modeling, computer vision
(image processing) and scientific visualization. It
teaches basic methods and algorithms (including
lighting models, virtual cameras, free-form surfaces,
pattern recognition) that students use in challenging
projects using powerful graphics hardware and latest
software libraries. Applications of computer graphics
can be found e. g. in technical quality assurance
systems, access control systems, 3D navigation
systems, computer games and in CAD / CAM-based
product development.



The major _Data Science_ deals with different aspects
of data management and analysis. Topics in the field
of management are in-depth knowledge of data
modelling and non-relational approaches to data
storage. For example, NoSQL database systems,
data warehouses, distributed relational DBMS, cloud
computing or the management of geospatial data are
discussed. In addition, architectures and algorithms
are treated that achieve high scalability and
availability through distribution and parallelization of
database systems, e. g. for big data applications. In
the area of data analysis, different approaches of
machine learning and data mining are discussed, with
a special focus on neural networks and deep learning.


The major _Complex Software Systems_ deals with the
professional development of extensive and complex
software systems. The students get to know current
concepts, principles, methods and processes of
software development. Thematically, for example, the
major includes various programming language
paradigms, test methods, design patterns, and
distributed software architectures. Furthermore, new
approaches in software development are presented,
esp. agile methods, domain specific languages,
complex event processing, cloud computing, mobile
computing, as well as methods from the field of
intelligent systems.


The major _IT Security_ is about the challenge of
protecting increasingly complex modern IT systems
from the growing threat posed by malicious attackers,
while keeping them efficient and usable. In this, the
major addresses all levels, from the network to the
computer system to the software application. The
students learn the theoretical basics and the current
methods of IT security. These include cryptographic
techniques such as encryption and digital signatures,
network security measures such as firewalls, system
security measures such as authentication and
authorization, and methods for developing secure
software.
