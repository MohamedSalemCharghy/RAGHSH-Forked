Call for Papers
**6th Event Education Symposium – Mastering Complexity:**
**Navigating Challenges in Event Education and Practice**

**11 June 2026, Hanover, Germany**
**Bachelor Event Management, Hanover University of Applied Sciences and Arts**

**"Mastering Complexity" - Understanding and Managing Complexity in the Event Industry**

The event industry is characterized by increasing complexity: climate change, digital transformation /
artificial intelligence, diversity in workforce / target audiences, and evolving security requirements are
fundamentally changing how events are planned, implemented and evaluated. Together, these
challenges create an environment that is frequently described as volatile, uncertain, complex, and
ambiguous (VUCA) and is challenging traditionally linear approaches to event management.

The **6th Event Education Symposium** invites academics, students, and practitioners to explore and
discuss the theme of "Mastering Complexity". We aim to examine how the event industry and event
education at universities can develop resilience, flexibility, and innovative approaches to address these
challenges.

Complexity in event management manifests itself among others through "wicked problems" (Wise et al.
2025) - issues that resist simple solutions due to their interconnected nature, diverse stakeholder
interests, and varying parameters. From managing the carbon footprint of large-scale events to
integrating AI-driven personalisation while ensuring data protection and meeting the needs of diverse
audiences and employees, event professionals face decisions where every solution creates new
challenges.

At the symposium, we want to discuss and understand how the event industry can respond and how
academic education of future professionals can prepare them for this reality.

**We welcome contributions on the following topics:**

**Cluster 1: Climate Action and Sustainability Transition**
The climate crisis demands transformative action from the event industry. Festivals, events, and trade
shows - along with event venues - face pressure regarding their CO ₂ emissions, resource consumption,
and environmental impact. At the same time, events provide a platform for exploring sustainable
innovations and initiating and promoting behavioural change.

**Cluster 2: Digital Transformation and Artificial Intelligence**
Artificial intelligence is rapidly transforming event planning, implementation, and evaluation. Through
predictive analytics, automated processes, and AI-driven personalisation and chatbots, these
technologies promise greater efficiency and improved visitor service. At the same time, they raise
fundamental questions about audience acceptance, skills requirements, legal and ethical boundaries,
and the value of human connection.

**Cluster 3: Security, Risk, and Resilience**
Recent years have demonstrated the vulnerability of events to a wide range of threats from pandemics
and terrorism to cyber-attacks and extreme weather events. Building resilient event ecosystems requires
new approaches to risk assessment, crisis management and stakeholder integration.


**Cluster 4: Complexity in Leadership, Diversity, and Stakeholder Management**
Events are temporary projects involving diverse stakeholders such as audiences and employees, who
often have heterogeneous interests and divergent timelines. Managing this complexity requires new
leadership approaches that embrace uncertainty rather than seeking to eliminate it.

**Cluster 5: Curriculum Development and Future Skills**
The rapid pace of industry change continually challenges event management education and training.
Curricula must prepare students for roles that may not yet exist while ensuring they continue to master
fundamental competencies.

We welcome the following **types of contributions** to the symposium:

- **Research presentations** : Original research findings from empirical studies, theoretical analyses, or
systematic literature reviews addressing symposium topics (presentation of 20 minutes plus 10
minutes Q & A).

- **Practitioner contributions** : Reflections from industry professionals on managing complexity in
event practice, with implications for education and training.

- **Young Researchers Forum** : Students are invited to present findings from their bachelor's or
master's theses, ongoing research projects, or innovative student-led projects.

- **Poster sessions** : Visual presentations of current research projects, curriculum innovations, or
practical initiatives for interactive discussion.

**Submission Guidelines:**

- Submit an extended abstract of maximum 2,000 words by 31 March 2026 to ees@hs-hannover.de

- All submitted contributions will be reviewed (peer-review).

- Accepted contributions will present their work at the symposium on 11 June 2026 in Hanover.

- We plan to publish all contributions in an edited anthology. Full papers of maximum 25 pages are
required by 30 September 2026.

**Who should contribute?**
We welcome contributions from academics and lecturers from event management, tourism and related
disciplines; students presenting thesis research or innovative projects, and practitioners willing to share
professional insights and engage in theory-practice dialogue.

The Event Education Symposium has been a forum for advancing university-based event management
education since 2014. This sixth edition continues our commitment to fostering dialogue between
teaching, research, and professional practice. The symposium is regularly organised by students from
the Bachelor Event Management programme at Hannover University of Applied Sciences and Arts as
part of a practical project module.
