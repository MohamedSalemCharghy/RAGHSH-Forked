Incoming-Interview



**Where were you staying while studying in**
**Hannover?**
I stayed mostly in the Papehof Student Residence in
Ricklingen, where my apartment was quite small but I
was prepared for that, and found it easier to make friends
at the student parties.


**Which was your favourite place in Hannover and**
**why?**
There are two places that are, at a draw, my ultimate
favorites: One is Old Kröpcke because the Cathedral is
stunning, and the various Vintage and Goth shops are my
highlights to explore, and the markets on a Saturday
morning! It’s colourful and fairytale like, and I made many
good deals that I still treasure! The second is the
Ricklinger Forests. During the Spring-Summer of 2018
seeing the snow, through to it becoming this peaceful
forests with flowers and lakes reflecting the skies that
changed colours.

**What did you do during Induction Week and would**
**you recommend it?**
I remember I was greedy! I wanted to try everything! I
would recommend the trampoline place and ice rink! It
was lively and it was full fun! It didn’t stop me crashing
onto the ground!


**What did you do in your spare/free time?**
At first, I was on my own because I didn’t know anybody,
and my classes were crammed. Near the end, I went
exploring, which helped me think and absorb myself
inside and out. When I slowly got to know people, it was
going to various parties and hanging-out making
pancakes, going to the beach, and I went to many places
and events. Gluten Free Cake at Coffee Time… Yes!

**After your semester at Hannover University, would**
**you recommend it to other students and if so, why / if**
**not, why not?**
I would recommend it to people who are prepared to
accept they are going to be different afterwards. When
you get home, there is this sort of Bilbo Baggins-like
distortion, where the life you used to live is an old skin.
You have the option to break from this, and people are
not going to get it unless they feel it for themselves. After
Erasmus, before doing the final year, sit out to absorb
this new identity, because being shoved back into the
“old life” will be painful as you apply the Lessons of
ERASMUS to your life slowly and not embrace it
properly. Just be prepared and learn each day and know
that there will be old things that will not benefit you after
you change. To embrace that change properly, is up to
you at the end of the day, and don’t you dare let anyone
take that away from you. Own it!

Incoming-Interview


**What were you surprised about, in regard to stereotypes, characteristics of Germans?**
By growing up half German/Brit while living in the UK… I honestly wasn’t sure what to expect. People are
people regardless of the land they walk on y’know? But growing up with the stereotypes is painful, and
being told that because I was too chatty or upfront, I wasn’t going to be welcomed, because I wasn’t “Cold”,
by other Erasmus Students. Boy were they wrong! Because what I saw were kind, welcoming, honestterrifyingly openly honest- good humored people, and I strangely related to them more and more in a
human way, rather than use “Happiness like a mask” or “Apologise all the time for being out of line”. It
made me proud that I was part German, and I owned it more in my identity.


**Buddy Topic: Who helped you find your way around the university, and did the same person help**
**you also in daily life, or someone different?**
My closest friend who was an ERASMUS student to Scotland, was a saint! I’m not gonna lie. She helped
me settle-in, and I stayed at her house where her mother made amazing food and introduced me to her
friends. This helped me meet various people, and joined a bunch of cool dudes in my accommodation
where we had beers and parties together. This made me more confident to end-up befriending my entire
department!

**Has studying in Hannover made you rethink any aspect of your future plans?**
It certainly opened doors and better understanding of who I want to be. How that there is an abundance of
potential beyond the ceiling. There was a rethink required before I came to whom I didn’t want to be, but I
just know I still need to figure how to find my potential, on my own terms.


**Which three key things will you take away with you from your studies in Hannover?**
What I have taken away from Hannover is, it’s okay not to be perfect. The world keeps moving and so do you.
You will learn the lessons that you need and will grow.


**What lasting impression will you have of Hannover?**
HEIMAT. Always.
