## **MANDATORY ON ARRIVAL:** **WHY IS THIS MANDATORY?**

**1.** **BSN-REGISTRATION**

If you move to the Netherlands from abroad, you must
register as a resident here. After registering you will
receive a Dutch citizen service number (BSN). You
need a BSN for the following things:


-To open a Dutch bank account
-To work
-To take out healthcare insurance
-To apply for governmental benefits


You will receive an email with an invitation for the
registration.


**2. PICK-UP RESIDENCE PERMIT**

After Saxion applied for your residence permit, you will
receive an email that it has been approved. In case you
have to go to the IND to let your biometrics be taken,
you will receive an email.
Once your residence permit is ready to be picked up,
you will also receive an email from us.


**We advise you to make an appointment as soon as**
**your residence permit has been approved.**

**MEDICAL CARE**

Medical Care is of high quality in the
Netherlands. Since medical costs for
treatment can be very high, it is very
important to be properly insured, especially
if you need specialist help from the hospital.
In the Netherlands you need to see the
Family doctor first before you can go to the
Hospital.


**INSURANCE**

There is a special insurance for (non-) EU
students. International Office will arrange
this insurance for you.
If you would like to arrange the insurance
yourself, arrange this either before coming
to The Netherlands or as soon as you arrive.
Sometimes it is possible to take out an
insurance policy through the educational
establishment at which you are a guest.
This also applies to other types of
insurance, like a third-party liability
insurance (W.A.-verzekering) or a fire and
theft insurance (brand/diefstalverzekering).


[Check out our video about insurance here.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E2TY3kegoo0&list=PLVxbxX3iRJ2QCPVQbMiktSAqzws7qJpBM)

Are you an EU student? Please double check
what it is you’re insured for.


Medical costs in The Netherlands can be
very high if not properly insured.

## **PUBLIC TRANSPORT**

For many internationals arriving in the Netherlands, Dutch transportation means one

thing: the bicycle. But while the Dutch have created the best cycling infrastructure in the


world, they’ve done some great things with their public transit system, too. So whether

you’re looking to take a train to Tilburg or a bus to Bloemendaal, it’s easy getting around


the country.



**THE OV-CHIPCARD**

The OV-chipkaart is the payment method for
public transport in the Netherlands. If you carry
an OV-chipkaart with you, you no longer need to
think about strip tickets or metro tickets. You
load credit onto the card in Euro or add a travel
product, such as a single journey, a monthly
pass for commuting or a season ticket.
