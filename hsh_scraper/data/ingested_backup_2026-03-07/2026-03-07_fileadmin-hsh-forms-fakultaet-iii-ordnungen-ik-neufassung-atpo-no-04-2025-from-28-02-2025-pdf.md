# **General part of the examination regulations** **for the Bachelor's and Master's degree programmes at** **Hannover University of Applied Sciences and Arts** **(ATPO-FIII 2024)** **Reading version** **Part One General Provisions** **§ 1** **Scope of application**

(1) These regulations contain cross-degree programme regulations for Bachelor's and Master's
degree programmes at Faculty III Media, Information and Design at Hannover University of
Applied Sciences and Arts. They apply to all degree programmes of the faculty that refer to these
Examination Regulations.


(2) In the examination regulations, the faculty regulates the subject-specific provisions the
subject-specific provisions for the respective degree programmes as well as the deviations from
this general section of the examination regulations. In this respect, the regulations in the
examination regulations of the respective degree programme take precedence over the
provisions in the general section. The examination regulations of the respective degree
programme apply as special parts of these examination regulations.

# **§ 2** **Purpose of the tests**


(1) The Bachelor's examination is the first professionally qualifying qualification of the degree
programme. The Bachelor's examination determines whether the candidate has acquired the indepth specialist knowledge required for the transition to professional practice, has an overview of
the subject-specific contexts and has the ability to work scientifically (and, if applicable, artistically)
and to apply scientific knowledge in a well-founded and reflective manner both in applicationoriented and in overarching social, economic and ecological contexts.


(2) The Bachelor's examination is preceded by the preliminary examination. It is intended to
determine whether the candidate has acquired the content-related and methodological
foundations of their specialisation and a systematic orientation in order to successfully continue
their studies.


(3) The Master's examination forms a further professional qualification of the degree
programme. The Master's examination determines whether the candidate has acquired the indepth specialist and methodological knowledge required for professional practice, has an
overview of interdisciplinary contexts and has the ability to work in management positions and
outstanding specialist positions on a scientific basis.

# **§ 3** **Audit Committee**


(1) Examination boards are formed to organise the examinations and to perform the tasks
assigned by these examination regulations. Examination boards ensure that the provisions of the
Lower Saxony Higher Education Act (NHG) and the relevant examination regulations are complied
with. The examination board or the body authorised by it shall keep the examination files.


(2) Examination boards of the faculties consist of seven members, four of whom are professors
and one member each from the group of academic and artistic staff, teaching staff for special
tasks, students and technical and administrative staff. The chair and deputy chair must be held by
professors; they and the other members of the Examination Board and their permanent deputies
are elected by the respective group representatives on the Faculty Council.


(3) In the case of inter-faculty degree programmes, the examination board of the lead faculty is
responsible. The appropriate participation of the cooperating faculty is ensured by the fact that
the chairperson of the examination board responsible for the content of the cooperating faculty is
entitled to vote as an additional member of the examination board responsible for the content of
the lead faculty in matters relating to the cross-faculty degree programme.


(4) Examination committees are commissions within the meaning of the Basic Regulations.
All genders should be given appropriate consideration when appointing members. The term
of office for members of the examination board is two years, that of student members is one
year.


(5) The Audit Committee is quorate if the majority of its members are present. It passes its
resolutions by a majority of the valid votes cast; abstentions are considered votes not cast. A
resolution is not passed if more than half of the voting members present cast an invalid vote or
abstain from voting.


(6) The Audit Committee shall adopt its own rules of procedure. Minutes shall be kept of the
meetings of the Audit Committee. The main topics of discussion and the resolutions of the Audit
Committee shall be recorded in the minutes.


(7) The Examination Committee may delegate powers to the Chair on a revocable basis;
deputisation is permitted. Rejecting decisions in appeal proceedings can be
may not be delegated. The Chair prepares and implements the resolutions of the Audit Committee.
He reports to the Audit Committee on these activities on an ongoing basis.


(8) The members of the Examination Committee have the right to attend the examinations as
observers.

(9) The meetings of the Audit Committee are not public. The members of the Audit Committee

# **§ 4** **Examiners and assessors**


(1) All persons at Hannover University of Applied Sciences and Arts who are authorised to
teach independently are examiners without special appointment (Section 3 (9) applies
accordingly). The Examination Board may appoint further examiners, provided that they
possess at least the qualification to be determined by the examination or an equivalent
qualification. If they are not in public service, they must be sworn to secrecy by the chairperson.
The same applies to assessors.


(2) The names of the examiners must be announced to the candidates at least two weeks before
the date of the respective examination. If the candidate waives the deadline, this must be
recorded. If the candidate attends the examination without complaining, a waiver shall be
assumed.


(3) Students may propose examiners for examinations. The suggestion does not constitute a
claim.


(4) Unless otherwise stated in the special section, examinations are assessed by an expert
examiner. This does not apply to the following examinations in accordance with Section 7 (1),
which are regulated separately: No. 2 (oral examination), No. 12 (Bachelor's/Master's thesis), No.
13 (colloquium), No. 14 (final oral examination).

# **§ 5** **Recognition of achievements and competences**


(1) Study achievements acquired at universities, including practical work experience, examination
achievements and credits (CR) in accordance with ECTS are recognised, provided equivalence
has been established. The same applies to previous examinations in the same or a related
degree programme. If the previous degree programme does not contain course content and
achievements that are the subject of the preliminary examination in accordance with these
regulations, recognition is possible subject to conditions.


(2) Equivalence shall be established if no significant difference can be demonstrated and
coursework, including practical work, examinations and credits, essentially correspond in content,
scope and requirements to those of the degree programme for which recognition is requested. No
schematic comparison is to be made, but rather an overall consideration and overall assessment
with regard to the significance of the achievements for the purpose of the examinations in
accordance with § 2. The equivalence of a foreign degree programme shall be determined on the
basis of the equivalence agreements approved by the Standing Conference of the Ministers of
Education and Cultural Affairs of the Länder and the German Rectors' Conference or other
intergovernmental agreements. If no agreements exist or further recognition is applied for, the
Examination Board decides on the equivalence. In order to clarify the factual and legal situation,
an opinion may be obtained from the Central Office for Foreign Education. Deviating recognition
provisions based on agreements with foreign universities remain unaffected.

(3) The student submits an application for recognition to the Examination Board. The Examination

(7) Paragraphs 1 to 5 also apply to multiple degree programmes for students at Hannover

# **§ 10** **Assessment of the examination performance and calculation of the module** **grade**


(1) The individual examinations are assessed by the respective examiners. Written examinations
must generally be assessed within one month of the respective examination.


(2) Notwithstanding paragraph 7, the following grades are to be used for the
assessment: 1.0, 1.3 = **very good** = a particularly outstanding performance
1.7, 2.0, 2.3 = **good** = performance significantly above average requirements 2.7, 3.0, 3.3 =

3.7, 4.0 = **sufficient** = a performance that meets the minimum requirements despite its

5 = **insufficient** = a performance that does not meet the minimum requirements due to significant
deficiencies.


(3) The examination is passed if it is assessed as at least "sufficient". If the examination is
assessed by two examiners, it is only passed if both assess the performance as at least
"sufficient". In the event of differing grades by two examiners or in the case of a peer
examination, the grade of the passed examination is calculated from the average of the individual
grades determined by the examiners. The reasons for the assessment decision, together with the
supporting considerations, must be provided in writing at the student's request, unless they are
provided at the same time as the assessment. The reasons must be included in the examination
file together with the examination paper. Paragraph 7 remains unaffected.

with an average of up to 1.5: **very good**

# **§ 12** **Certificates, notifications and attestations**


(1) If the preliminary examination, Bachelor's or Master's examination has been passed or finally
failed (§ 19 or § 24), the Chair of the Examination Board will issue a notification. A decision on
individual grades will not be made.


(2) A bilingual certificate (German and English) must be issued immediately, if possible within one
month, to confirm that the Bachelor's or Master's examination has been passed; a Diploma
Supplement and, on request, a Transcript of Records in German and English will be attached to
the Bachelor's or Master's certificate. The date of the certificate must be the date on which the
examination was passed.


(3) The degree achieved (see also § 1 Special Section) is certified with the Bachelor's or
Master's degree certificate. The name of the degree programme is listed in two languages
(German and English).


(4) Students who leave the university as a result of a final failure will be issued with a transcript
of records containing the examinations taken and their assessment. Students who leave the
university without a degree for other reasons will be issued with a transcript of records upon
request.


(5) The inclusion of further achievements (additional modules) in the certificate or the transcript of
records is permitted. These are not taken into account when calculating the overall grade.

# **Third part** **Bachelor's and Master's examination** **§ 20** **Type and scope**


The Bachelor's examination and the Master's examination are taken during the course of study. The
type and scope of the examinations to be taken are set out in Annexes B1, B2 and B3 to the
respective special section. Admission is governed by § 6.

# **§ 21** **Bachelor's or Master's thesis**


(1) Admission to the Bachelor's or Master's thesis is governed by the special
section of the examination regulations for the respective degree programme.


(2) The Bachelor's or Master's thesis should show that the candidate is able to work
independently on a topic from the subject area of the degree programme using scientific methods
within a specified period of time.


(3) The Bachelor's or Master's thesis can be completed in the form of group work. The contribution
of the individual candidate to be assessed as an examination performance must be clearly
definable and assessable on the basis of the specification of sections, page numbers or other
objective criteria and must fulfil the requirements according to para. 2.


(4) The first examiner should be a professor from one of the faculties significantly involved in the
degree programme. The Examination Board may also appoint other full-time lecturers from the
participating faculties or professors from other faculties as first examiners, provided that the
second examiner is a professor from the participating faculties.
During the preparation of the thesis, the candidate is supervised by the first examiner.


(5) The topic to be worked on is determined by the first examiner after hearing the candidate. At
the candidate's request, the examination board shall ensure that the candidate receives a topic in
good time. The binding issue of the topic is made by the chair of the examination committee; the
issue must be recorded. The examiners, the topic to be worked on and the start of work are
documented when the topic is issued.


(6) The topic can only be returned once and only within the first third of the processing
time.

(7) When submitting the Bachelor's or Master's thesis, the candidate must declare in text form that
he/she has written the thesis - in the case of a group thesis, the appropriately labelled part of the
thesis - independently and has not used any sources or aids other than those specified. By order
of the examination board, the declaration must be made in writing or as a written affirmation in lieu
of an oath. When examining work using software, the candidate's copyright must be respected.


(8) The Bachelor's or Master's thesis must be submitted to the Chair of the Examination Board
by the deadline; the submission date must be recorded. In individual cases, the Examination
Board may extend the completion time appropriately, usually by up to one month, upon justified
request.


(9) As a rule, the work must be assessed by both examiners within one month of submission
in accordance with Section 10 (2) to (4), (6).

# **§ 22** **Colloquium; final oral examination**


(1) In the colloquium, the candidate must demonstrate in a discussion of the Bachelor's or
Master's thesis that he or she is able to work independently on interdisciplinary and problemrelated issues from the field of this specialisation on a scientific basis and to deepen the results
of the work in a specialist discussion.


(2) The prerequisite for admission to the colloquium is that the requirements according to § 20 are
fulfilled - the Examination Board decides on exceptions - and the Bachelor's or Master's thesis has
been provisionally assessed by the examiners as at least "sufficient". As a rule, the colloquium
should be held within six weeks of submission of the Bachelor's or Master's thesis.


(3) The colloquium is conducted jointly by the examiners of the Bachelor's or Master's thesis as
an individual or group examination. Otherwise, Section 7 (4) and (15) and Section 8 apply
accordingly.


(4) The special section (Annex B2 or B3) of the respective degree programme indicates whether the
Bachelor's or Master's thesis and the colloquium are combined as one examination or as two
examinations (possibly with different weighting factors) in a joint module or whether there are two
separate modules (with separate credits).


(5) The colloquium may be waived if both examiners and the candidate sign a declaration waiving
the colloquium and the Bachelor's thesis or Master's thesis and colloquium are defined as a joint
examination (Annex B2 or B3 to the respective special section). If the examination is recognised
separately, the colloquium must be carried out and passed.


(6) The special sections may provide for a final oral examination to take the place of the
colloquium. In the final oral examination, the candidate must demonstrate that he/she is able to
independently represent and deepen the scientific foundations of the degree programme in a
specialist discussion on an exemplary topic using scientific reflection. The final oral examination
can be conducted as an individual examination or as a group examination. Otherwise, Section 7 (4)
and (15) and Section 8 apply accordingly. Section 6 applies to admission to the final oral
examination.

# **§ 23** **Repetition of the Bachelor's or Master's thesis**


(1) The Bachelor's or Master's thesis may be repeated once if it has been assessed as
"insufficient" or is deemed to have been assessed as "insufficient"; a second repetition is
excluded. Returning the topic in accordance with Section 21 (5) when repeating the Bachelor's or
Master's thesis is permitted, but only if this option has not already been utilised for the first thesis.


(2) The new topic of the Bachelor's or Master's thesis will be issued within a reasonable period
of time, usually within three months of the assessment of the first thesis.

# **§ 24** **Overall result of the audit**


(1) The Bachelor's or Master's examination is passed if all examinations of the compulsory
modules and the required compulsory elective modules listed in Annexes B1 and B2 or B3 to the
respective Special Section have been graded at least "sufficient" or "passed" and the required
number of credits has been earned.


(2) The overall grade of the Bachelor's or Master's examination is calculated from the average of
the weighted grades for the module examinations (compulsory and compulsory elective
modules). The special section of the respective degree programme regulates in detail which
modules are taken into account and the weighting with which they are included in the overall
grade. If more compulsory elective modules have been passed than are required to achieve the
total number of credits, students can choose which compulsory elective modules are used to
calculate the overall grade. Compulsory elective modules that have been passed in excess of
the required number and elective modules are not included in the overall grade. § Section 10 (4)
and (6) apply accordingly.


(3) The Bachelor's or Master's examination is definitively failed if a required module
examination or an examination performance or the Bachelor's or Master's thesis is
assessed as "insufficient" or "failed" or is assessed as "insufficient" or "failed" and there is
no longer an opportunity to repeat the examination.

For students who are already studying on a degree programme at the time these amendments
come into force, Section 19 (1) in the previous version (ATPO 2015) shall continue to apply to this
degree programme.
