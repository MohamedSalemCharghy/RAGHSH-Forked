## What is LABi³?
[ Description ](https://www.hs-hannover.de/internationales/weltweit-vernetzt/internationale-projekte/labi3 "Description")
The LABi³ project is an innovative, interdisciplinary and international study programme on the topic of sustainable circular economy.
_Innovative_
We experiment with inventive teaching methods (e.g. design thinking) and conceptual designs and digital and hybrid teaching formats.
_Interdisciplinary_
The world has become so complex that we believe that it needs different perspectives to solve today’s problems. We explore how different academic disciplines and their respective industrial partners can all find a common ground.
_International_
We cooperate with four international partner universities and offer scholarships for students and teaching staff.
LABi³ Project aims for:
  * Development and set up of international practice-based study programmes
  * Establishment and further development of interactions with national and international partners
  * Establish and intensify mobility between all academic and industrial partners
  * Create an international master’s programme


The project is supported by the DAAD.

## Photobook 1.0 - 2021/2022
LABi³ organised an international teaching event in the winter semester 2021/22. The prototype for this teaching project was developed together with international partners in the first kick-off meeting in autumn of 2020. More than 50 students from Albania, Colombia, Finland, Hungary, and Germany illustrated country-specific examples of sustainable circular economy in the form of a photobook. Equipped with their camera, teams of students documented examples of circular economy in their immediate surroundings and analyse the specific concept and deduce common factors of circular economy. Sustainability, drafts of the photobook as well as completed chapters of it were discussed and presented in joint online teaching events; especially the interaction and discussion were facilitated live by digital communication platforms. A second round with an adapted concept will be carried out in the winter semester 2022/23.

## Photobook 2.0 - 2022/23
The second round of the Photobook project took place in winter semester 2022/23. On the basis of student evaluations from the first round, the project was adapted and further developed. In particular, the students' wish to work more in interdisciplinary and international groups was implemented. A total of 28 students from 6 different universities were able to choose from 6 different topics and were divided into groups.
These groups were supervised by one or two teachers from the different universities. An interdisciplinary and intercultural dialogue took place, which contributed to the successful implementation of the project, the presentation of the project results and the creation of illustrated chapters on the topic of sustainable circular economy.

## Research and Learning Development 
[ Hybrid Work ](https://www.hs-hannover.de/internationales/weltweit-vernetzt/internationale-projekte/labi3 "Hybrid Work")
Hybrid working and teaching is the combination of students, teachers, and partners participating together in person and online. This format widens the possibilities for teaching and collaboration. LABi3 established the technical requirements and digital formats for hybrid working and teaching in 2019/2020.
The first hybrid international workshop was held in 2020 with participants from all of our partner universities as well as several faculties and institutions of the Hanover University of Applied Sciences and Arts. The participants experienced the structures of hybrid formats and realised its potential for project conception, reconciliation, and innovative teaching.
[ Lab Work and Practice ](https://www.hs-hannover.de/internationales/weltweit-vernetzt/internationale-projekte/labi3 "Lab Work and Practice")
Lab work and practice courses in LABi3 aim to expand students’ knowledge and promote the practice transfer of their know-how to problem-solving on the topic of sustainable circular economy. Interdisciplinary thinking and social competence of students are trained within LABi3 practice-oriented courses.
International and German students took part in a LABi3 practice course, in which students independently developed research projects to improve sustainability processes in the field of food technology. Students took responsibility for their own work in literature research, definition of the research question, project planning, and experimental implementation. The course was held with the design thinking method and required deep communication using technical language in English. During implementation of the self-developed research experiment, students were given technical and practical training in the dairy technology centre and the laboratories of the Hochschule Hannover. Scientific writing and the presentation of results were practised through the online presentation of the project.
[ Interdisciplinary ](https://www.hs-hannover.de/internationales/weltweit-vernetzt/internationale-projekte/labi3 "Interdisciplinary")
Interdisciplinary work is the means to respond to the complex ecological, economical, and social challenges for the development of a sustainable future. LABi3’s goal is to initiate interdisciplinary teaching events, courses, and study programmes for students with multidisciplinary backgrounds to elaborate on integrative solutions for the current problems and barriers on the way to local, national and worldwide sustainable development.
Concepts for a sustainable university were developed across faculties with more than 50 participants from Hochschule Hannover in a LABi3 open space workshop in spring 2020. In autumn 2020, more than 30 representatives of the international LABi3 partner universities, as well as faculties and organisational units of Hochschule Hannover developed a wide range of interdisciplinary teaching formats to educate international students on the topic of sustainable circular economy. These formats are planned to be implemented in an interdisciplinary teaching event in 2021 in close consultation with our international partners.
[ Interculturally ](https://www.hs-hannover.de/internationales/weltweit-vernetzt/internationale-projekte/labi3 "Interculturally")
The international teaching programmes developed in LABi3 aim to invite students from different countries to participate in innovative courses with a focus on **interaction** , **communication** , and **independence**. The students bring in different cultural backgrounds into LABi3 courses, which affects the common working atmosphere and opens up an integrative view on the complex topic of sustainable development. Country-specific examples and application solutions of circular economy broaden the perspectives of the students and they further gain insight into its worldwide importance.
The background of our incoming students is extremely diverse: Albania, Columbia, Finnland and Hungary not only in terms of languages, but also in terms of experience and concrete scientific background. We consider this a big source of learning. This source enables us to **discover together** and learn not just from university staff but in equalmeasure from the fellow students, which is an asset as a basis for lifelong learning.
We started with the discovery of the unknown culture by writing a “Diary of Foreignness” by exchanging openly about the common and differencing points of our home cultures.
## Innovative Teamwork and Teaching - Design Thinking
The method of design thinking facilitates setting free the innovative potential in everyone: Challenges are examined from the perspective of a specific target group to comprehensively illuminate underlying facts and possibilities. Subsequently, problem-solving begins: Creative techniques enable everyone to think outside the box, make new connections, and turn abstract ideas into reality. Problem-solving prototypes are designed and tested adequately. Whenever tasks become complex and different groups of people are involved, the design thinking process provides immense added value.

## Partners
[ Agricultural University of Tirana (AUT), Albania ](https://www.hs-hannover.de/internationales/weltweit-vernetzt/internationale-projekte/labi3 "Agricultural University of Tirana \(AUT\), Albania")
Agricultural University of Tirana, Albania is the largest graduate, postgraduate, research and training centre for professionals and specialists in the field of agriculture and environment, especially for crop production, animal production; horticulture; plant protection; marine biology and aquaculture; natural resource management; agrarian economy; agricultural policies; integrated rural development; enterprise management; marketing; finance & accounting; agritourism, biotechnology and food; beverage industry; forestry; wood industry; veterinary medicine and public health.
At AUT, we define sustainable agriculture as production that delivers public goods and generates adequate family income, while at the same time contributes to key environmental goals, including the protection of natural and cultural resources and the achievement of successful climate change mitigation and adaptation. Topics of sustainability and sustainable circular economy are addressed as cross-cutting issues and are increasingly reflected in our curricula.
In the course of the EU accession process, we are obliged and committed to apply and adapt EU standards according to the Common Agricultural Policy (CAP) aiming at sustainable food production, resource efficiency and avoidance of food waste.
[ German Jordanian University (GJU), Jordan ](https://www.hs-hannover.de/internationales/weltweit-vernetzt/internationale-projekte/labi3 "German Jordanian University \(GJU\), Jordan")
The German-Jordanian University (GJU) is a public university located in Mushaqar. It was founded in 2005 by a Royal Decree, in accordance with a memorandum of understanding reached between the Ministry of Higher Education and Scientific Research of the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan and the Federal Ministry of Education and Research of the Federal Republic of Germany. In August 2004, a founding committee began up its activities in Jordan. The committee was in close cooperation with the Ministry of Higher Education and Scientific Research. 
The University was modelled on the German applied-sciences model, characterized by their focus on putting knowledge into practice and on promoting knowledge transfer. We aim to play a significant role in promoting links between Jordan and Europe, particularly Germany. By taking advantage of the best educational practices in both Jordan and Germany, the University has positioned itself as a leader in its field.
The University's focus is on providing undergraduate and graduate programs of the highest quality. We offer over 20 programs, with a student body of around 5,000, drawn predominantly from Jordan, but also including international students. We take pride in our student-centered environment for learning and believe that quality education is founded upon close associations between faculty and students.
[ Óbuda University, Hungary ](https://www.hs-hannover.de/internationales/weltweit-vernetzt/internationale-projekte/labi3 "Óbuda University, Hungary")
**Environmental Engineering BSc education in the Environmental Engineering Institute of Rejtő Sándor Faculty of Light Industry and Environmental Engineering at the Óbuda University**
The aim of the education is to qualify the students to be environmental engineers who are familiar with and able to perform tests to characterise and assess the quantity and quality of environmental elements, hazards, technologies and operations by state-of-the-art measuring instruments.
Responsible environmental awareness is the engineers’ key competency in general. Here at the Óbuda University, the environmental engineering students are armed with fundamental knowledge and understanding of global environmental and natural issues on the base of scientific evidences.
It is important to develop complex knowledge, - both theoretical and practical work - to ensure practice-oriented education via project work.
During the project work trainings, our studentscooperate with engineers, industrial players and decision makers involved in the development and application of different production technologies to develop the given technology in terms of environment protection.
Students of the programme participate creatively in engineering work based on their multidisciplinary skills gained by their studies.
The project work qualifies our students to solve tasks relating to water, soil, air, radiation, and noise protection problems, as well as those of waste treatment.
Depending on the topic of the given project work, students can work with global environmental problems or they can choose a more practical task as environmental assessment or audit as well as dealing with special areas of waste recycling, waste water treatment or soil remediation.
Our students are equipped with a specific understanding of environmental problems of the Light Industry, they are trained via project work to be able to deal with the tasks of Environmental Public Administration or they can specialise in the Green Energy Industry.

Various research projects have been and are being carried out here on the following subjects: sustainable nutrient and material cycles, innovative biomaterials and packaging, and sustainable food systems.
