Dear exchange students,

Please find attached information concerning courses that are available for our incoming students. In
the field of **Business Administration**, we currently have a range of courses on the 3 [rd] and 4 [th] year level
available in English, as listed below.

We would like to warmly recommend our Spring Welcome Weeks to you. They will take place on campus
Linden from 16 February until 27 February 2026. The welcome weeks offer you the chance of a great
start in an international environment. You will meet fellow students, build lasting friendships and get a
taste of university life while earning credits and gaining important competencies.
Details will be provided by the Incoming Team on time.

If you wish to take part in the German Intensive Course (16 February until 27 February 2026),
registration is required. You will receive an e-mail by Ms. Karin Sziedat, [karin.sziedat@hs-hannover.de](mailto:karin.sziedat@hs-hannover.de)
with information how to sign up.

**Please note: if you want to take part in the course “German as a foreign language A1” during the**
**semester, it is necessary either to have basic knowledge of the German language or to attend the**
**German Intensive Course beforehand.**

In addition to that, all guest students are invited to join our multiple language classes at different levels
(such as German, French, Italian, Spanish, and - of course - English).

Please note: The majority of credit points should be gained through courses of the Department of
Business Administration. **It is not possible to do ONLY language courses.**

Course title **Presentation Techniques**


Course content This course includes: research into an academic topic in detail¸ preparing
and delivering short oral assignments; working in small groups for in-class
tasks; learning to structure a presentation effectively; giving constructive
feedback to peers.

Learning outcomes On completion of this module the student should be able to: self-organize
and structure an oral presentation in English; present confidently in front
of a small group; arrange information effectively in speech and on
presentation slides; discuss and answer questions on their topics and
critique each other; effectively deal with anxiety before public speaking
occasions; use appropriate vocabulary, verb tenses and sentence structure
when giving a presentation.

Credits 3 ECTS
Degree course International Business Studies
Semester Fall and Spring


Course title **Intercultural Management Training (ICM)**


Course content Concepts of Culture (Hofstede; Trompenaars & Hampden-Turner),
personality (Five Factor Model), intercultural competence, intercultural
group formation, group development and conflict management, basics of
intercultural communication and negotiation. Application of theories and
methods in group projects, case studies and business simulations.

Learning outcomes Students understand the scientific dimensions of culture and are able to
apply them in a project-oriented working environment in groups of mixed
nationalities. They acquire the competence to analyse, assess and adapt to
different cultures as well as to find creative, innovative solutions for a
culture-based assignment. Students are able to react appropriately to
stressful situations with time and group pressure by reconciling conflicts
and cultural dilemmas in their intercultural working groups. Thus, they
create a positive work environment. This corresponds to a basic level of
intercultural competencies.

Credits 6 ECTS
Degree course International Business Studies, Business Administration
Semester Fall

Course title **International Business Environment**


Course content Relevance of the macroeconomic business environment in strategic
management-sources for international macroeconomic research,
international macroeconomic rankings and development reports (HDI,
Doing Business, etc.). Major global organizations in international business
(UN, OECD, WTO, World Bank, IMF, IFC, etc.). PESTEL and SWOT analysis.

able to analyze major macroeconomic risks and trends from a

order to pro-actively consider these risks and trends.

Course title **IMA Strategic International Management**


Course content Sustainable management of Transnational Corporations based on the
holistic EFQM Model: defining purpose and strategy based on
international stakeholders’ needs, building corporate culture and leading
change, engaging stakeholders, creating sustainable value, driving
performance and transformation while managing risks and opportunities,
achieving sustainable results and reviewing the organisation’s
management model.

Learning outcomes Students understand the special requirements of the development,
implementation and review of sustainable international business
strategies from a holistic management perspective. They are able to apply
this knowledge to organisations in different industries, to select suitable
state-of-the-art tools and techniques and to critically reflect possible
outcomes. Students know how to examine organisational approaches and
how to assess an organisation’s performance and maturity based on the
EFQM RADAR logic. They can create own, creative approaches for predefined business settings and management aspects in such a way that this
would lead to an excellent assessment of the organisation’s management
system and sustainability. Due to the use of case studies, work groups and
other active learning methods with international participants, students are
not only able to characterise cultural differences, but also to modify their
own communication patterns and identify successful group cooperation
strategies against the background of internationally accepted ethical value
systems.

Credits 6 ECTS
Degree course International Business Studies, Business Administration
Semester Fall and Spring


Course title **IMA Sustainable Development from a Management Perspective**


Course content History and current relevance of sustainable development,
industrialization and globalization, major socio-ecological challenges in our
world (incl. planetary boundaries, etc.), design-objectives and -principles
for sustainable development, alternative approaches to sustainable
development for different fields/industries (e. g. food-production, energy,
transport/mobility, etc.).

Learning outcomes Students are able to critically evaluate the history and managerial
implications (major risks and opportunities) of industrialization,
globalization and the current growth-paradigm as well as the symptoms
and roots of contemporary challenges in the macro-environment of
international management. Students can contrast the concept and
necessity of sustainable development against currently dominating
approaches of the “green economy“ as well as against alternative
approaches to management for sustainable development (e. g. steadystate, etc.). They can design truly sustainable business solutions along both
regional and international lines of business in various fields of our society.

Course title **IMA Selected Topics of International Management**


Course content Recent theoretical concepts of selected aspects with high relevance for a
sustainable international management: selected methods and tools for
improving management decisions as well as driving change and innovation
in international environments, case studies and exercises dealing with
selected key challenges of international management in different
industries, active and agile learning methods applied in an intercultural
setting to ensure the develop-ment of solutions to real organisational
challenges from diverse perspectives.

Learning outcomes Students have a profound working knowledge of selected issues in
international management. They are able to apply recent methods and
tools to organisations in differentindustries in order to find appropriate
managerial responses to typical challenges in inter-national business
operations. Possible outcomes can be reflected. Students are able to
analyse a realistic organisational setting and its respective challenges based
on a pre-defined assignment. They can develop and design appropriate and
innovative solutions for individual challenges in an international group
setting, which further enhances their problem-solving abilities. Through the
use of case studies, real company assignments, projects or other
participative learning methods, their English communication skills as well as
their group building skills will be enhanced.

Credits 6 ECTS

Course title **Business Ethics**


Course content History and terminology of business ethics, philosophical approaches to
business ethics, guidelines and frameworks for action in business ethics,
fields and instruments of corporate governance, stakeholder management,
ethics across different business functions, CSR vs. sustainable
management, instruments to analyse and design sustainable
managementness.

Learning outcomes Students can name the major milestones of the history of business ethics.
They are able to discuss the differences between the most relevant
philosophical approaches to business ethics. They can present examples
for dilemmas in different business functions and at different levels of
business decision making. Students are able to analyse and critically
evaluate a company's sustainability status and to develop specific
recommendations to further advance management efforts in this regard.

recent theories and new leadership. Leading Diverse Teams: Aspects of
diversity, intercultural differences based on Trompenaars/HampdenTurner’s dilemma framework and the GLOBE study, the Five Factor Model of

organisational environment and are able to choose possible approaches for

different cultures and personalities and their influence on leadership issues
can be appraised and considered. Also, the influence of leaders on their
team member’s motivation and well-being can be critically reviewed.
Students are able to evaluate typical issues of diverse teams based on reallife experiences and reflect upon their own reactions and behaviour
realistically. Appropriate individual learning goals concerning intercultural
and leadership competencies can be set and fitting steps to acquire the
needed competencies taken. A broad range of expressions for personal
learnings can be individually developed, taking a multitude of different
media into account. Students are able to reflect accurately on their
experiences, achievements and challenges. Due to the use of group work

Course title **International Sustainable Corporate Finance**


Course content Analysing the financial position of a company with respect to foreign
currency challenges, calculating the (incremental) free-cash-flows in
domestic currency from an international investment, quotations of foreign
exchange rates, International Parity Conditions of exchange rates, financial
hedging instruments and strategies

Learning outcomes Students are able to evaluate Annual Statements with reference to business
challenges resulting from international finance. They have knowledge in
quotations of exchange rates and are able to apply those on various quoting
problems. Using international parity conditions, students are able to
develop own assessments on exchange rate movements. Students know
instruments in order to manage international cash flows and are able to
design own hedging strategies on simple problems. Furthermore they are
able to talk about and write about topics in Finance in English and to
analyze international exchange rate movements with reference to global
economy developments.

Credits 6 ECTS
Degree course International Business Studies, Business Administration
Semester Fall and Spring

Course title **Global Economics**


Course content The course is concerned with the topic of globalization, i.e. the world-wide
integration of goods, services, and capital. Thus, we will be dealing both
with the trade and the monetary aspects of the economic interaction
between countries. We will be covering basic theoretical models and
concepts as well as many real-world applications. We will also relate the
topics covered in the course to recent events that have affected the world
economy. Throughout the course, we will look at these issues through the
lens of economics (rather than business administration). Tentative course
outline: 1. Introduction 2. Basic facts about world trade and globalization
3. Comparative advantage and the gains from trade 4. Resources and
international trade 5. External economies of scale and international trade
6. Firms in the global economy 7. International trade policy 8. National
income accounting and the balance of payments 9. Exchange rates and the
foreign exchange market. The course will include an excursion to Berlin.

Learning outcomes Students have a good understanding of trade patterns in the world and the
process of eceonmic globalization. They know the main theories of
international trade and can identify the winners and losers of trade
liberalization. Students are able to analyze real-world trade data and can
relate them to theoretical concepts.
